Zvi Mowshowitz on Pausing AI

Jaromír Svoboda (written with Claude) · 2026-10-24

In one paragraph. Zvi’s goal has not moved since 2023: nobody should build superintelligence until we know how to do it without losing control, and the right way to stop it is international coordination. What moved is his judgement of what can be done now. In 2023 he was glad to see a six-month pause proposed but did not sign the letter. Through 2025 he said a unilateral pause would not help. In 2026 he argued for “pacing the frontier” (building the ability to slow down on demand) and, after OpenAI’s internal models broke into HuggingFace in July 2026, said acting alone might now be justified.

What he wants

  • Don’t build superintelligence yet. “We should not build superintelligence until such time as that changes, and the risk of everyone dying as a result … is very low.” He signed FLI’s Statement on Superintelligence (2025-10-24), and in 2023 the CAIS extinction-risk statement.
  • Coordination is the end state. “The first best solution is still that we all coordinate to ensure no one tries to build superintelligence until we are in a much better position to do so” (IABIED review, 2025-09-12). “Ultimately, global coordination is The Way” (We Must Pace The Frontier, 2026-09-14).
  • The ability to stop, before it is needed. “What we want is not to pause or slow down now, but to build the ability to pause or slow down on demand” (AI #172, 2026-06-11). Concretely: a “flat limit on how much compute can go to frontier model training, designed to not bind any but the very top labs, would be ideal” (The Curve Bends You, 2026-10-07), plus transparency, testing, and “requiring individual labs to pause frontier model training … if they cannot pass such requirements” (2026-07-29).
  • He calls p(doom) “highly useful shorthand”: “I typically say 60% (p=0.6) when asked” (AI #42, 2023-12-14).

How his position moved

When What he said
Mar 2023: FLI six-month pause letter Welcomed it as groundwork: “I do not expect much chance of the pause happening, but would be thrilled if it did” (post). He did not sign; in 2025 he wrote that being happy the letter existed “was a mistake. I was wrong.”
Apr 2023: Yudkowsky in TIME Endorsed international limits on training-run size (post).
2023–2025: what is achievable Backed the 10²⁶ FLOP reporting threshold, California’s SB 1047 (“it is a good bill, sir”), New York’s RAISE Act (“insufficient on its own … I strongly support this bill”) and SB 53. Accepted lab Responsible Scaling Policies only as a complement to regulation: “I am more optimistic about getting to a full pause before it is too late, but it seems highly unlikely within the next few years” (RSPs, 2023-12-05).
Sep 2025: If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies “Under anything like current conditions, it seems highly unwise to build it”, but “a unilateral pause at this time would not help matters.”
Mar–Jun 2026 “I do not believe we should pause now. I do strongly believe we should be laying a foundation … such that we have the means to do so” (AI #161). In June: “I agree you can’t do unilateral slowing down.”
Jul–Aug 2026: after the HuggingFace hack Before it he would have said “No to forcing labs or everyone to do this”; after it, “OpenAI in particular may need to halt and catch fire.” “I do not think a full pause is warranted at this time, but that could quickly change,” and “plausibly we are now entering the phase where it becomes so dangerous to continue that it is better to pause individually, even without coordination” (The Pacing of the Frontier, 2026-08-10).
Sep–Oct 2026 “As things are we would be better off acting unilaterally, including because our Chinese competition is fast following. If we slowed down they would be slowed down as well” (We Must Pace The Frontier). And a warning: “Pacing the Frontier, on its own, by default only gets you killed slower.”

What he opposes

  • He rejects the China race as a reason for no rules, arguing that a US slowdown, with strong chip export controls, slows China too; “We should not sell highly capable Nvidia H200 chips to China” (2025-12-09).
  • Accelerationism of the kind that treats “any attempt to steer or slow such building” as tyranny, or human extinction as acceptable (2023-12-06).
  • Federal pre-emption without a substitute. He called the 2025 attempt to ban state AI laws “the insane attempted AI moratorium” (2025-07-01).
  • Self-regulation as the plan. “Self-regulation as a plan is indeed rather insane. It still is better than none at all” (2026-09-30).

On Nathan Labenz’s podcast

Zvi is a regular guest on The Cognitive Revolution. Thirteen published transcripts (July 2023 – September 2026) tell the same story as the blog, in looser words:

  • 2023: a limit that falls over time. “I think the ideal limitation regime is some limit that I would set right now to be maybe 10 to the 26 myself or something like that, then yes, it decreases over time … until such time as we’re convinced that we have some way to contain” it (2023-09-29). And: “people who are saying, no, we have to stop training bigger, more powerful models, have definitely made their point.”
  • 2025: against restraint by one side alone. Asked for his p(doom): “my p(doom) has gone up to 0.7” (2025-04-21). When Nathan suggested there might be virtue in “unilaterally not pursuing things like weaponization”, Zvi answered: “I think you’re just shooting yourself in the foot.”
  • 2026: coordination, and what pacing means. “We can’t really stop without a full international agreement to stop” (2026-06-21). Pacing means more than delaying releases: “we’re talking about actively restricting, like, levels of training runs and ability to, like, develop the models including internally if it comes to that” (2026-08-05). On activists, he pointed out that he had listed PauseAI US “two years in a row in my big nonprofits post as a recommended charity” (2026-03-19).

He did not call for an immediate full pause on any of the episodes.

How this was checked

Every quotation above was matched word for word against the text of the linked post or transcript. For blog posts, each was checked to be in Zvi’s own voice rather than in a passage he quotes from someone else (his posts quote others at length). For podcasts, each was checked against the speaker turn it sits in; one transcript (2025-04-21) swaps its speaker labels in the second half, so those turns were attributed by content (who asks, who answers). Transcripts are machine-made, so podcast quotes may contain small transcription errors. The selection is ours: about forty posts from March 2023 to October 2026 chosen by topic, and the thirteen Cognitive Revolution episodes with Zvi that have transcripts. It is not his full output.
